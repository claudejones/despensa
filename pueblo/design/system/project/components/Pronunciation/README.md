A pronunciation row shows how one Spanish word is said: its syllables, which one is stressed, a sound-it-out spelling and a speaker button.

## When to use

- The pronunciation drawer, opened with the Abc button on any screen that shows Spanish.
- The phrasebook.
- Beside the target line in a say-it-aloud challenge.

## When not to use

- Listening challenges, where reading the word would defeat the task.
- Giving meaning. It never shows the English.

## Anatomy

- Syllable chips in `word` style on `cal-low`; the stressed syllable is `ink` with `card` text.
- Sound-it-out spelling in `label`, the stressed part in capitals.
- Speaker icon button.
- In the drawer: one row for each Spanish word on screen, tiles first, then the line just spoken.

## Placement

In a bottom sheet on a phone, and a side sheet on a wide screen, where it can stay open beside the challenge.

## States

- Rest.
- Speaking: the chips light up one at a time as the word is said.

## Motion

Each chip takes the fast effects spring as it is spoken.

## Do and don't

- Do: Keep it free. It tells how a word sounds, not what it means.
- Do: Offer a slower speed.
- Don't: Mark stress with colour alone; the sound-it-out spelling capitalises it too.
- Don't: Use the five job colours for the chips.

## Accessibility

- Each speaker button names its word.
- Stress is shown by tone and by capitals.
- Chips are text and can be read by a screen reader as one word.

## What you provide

For each word: its syllables, the index of the stressed one, the sound-it-out spelling and the text to speak.

## Source

Pueblo original. Uses Material 3 colour roles, the corner radius scale and the Expressive springs.
